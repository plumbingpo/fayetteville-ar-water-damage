Maintenance Tips for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Before anything goes wrong, store the phone number for an emergency restoration crew.
  • Wrap plumbing in cold, unheated parts of the house so a hard freeze cannot crack it.
  • Being able to find the electrical panel quickly means wet areas can be de-energized without risk.
  • Going away longer than a few days? Close the main water valve first.
  • Find your main water shutoff today and make sure everyone in the house can close it.
  • A sump pump should be tried out before storms arrive, ideally with a backup battery installed.

Why Emergency Water Damage Restoration Is Not a DIY Job

Mopping the puddle in front of you is rarely the real challenge during an emergency. What takes skill is figuring out where everything else has soaked in. Baseboards, wall cavities and the space beneath flooring all take on water in minutes. Our team uses moisture meters and thermal imaging to find it, so the wet areas you cannot see are treated the same night instead of being found weeks later as a smell or a stain.

Our night crew arrives with gear homes simply do not have, including commercial air movers, properly sized dehumidifiers and truck-mounted extractors. The crew is trained to stay safe near contaminated water and wet wiring, and from hour one it records the loss in photos and moisture readings. That record helps when you file a claim. You get one team that handles the emergency and stays with the job until the home is dry.

Signs It Is Time for Emergency Water Damage Restoration

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • You have no way to locate or get to the shutoff while water continues to pour.
  • Trapped water overhead has made part of the ceiling bulge or sag.
  • Pooled water is creeping over the floor into neighboring rooms or onto the staircase.
  • Sewage has backed up, or the water is dark with a foul odor.
  • The breaker box, a running appliance or cords along the floor sit in the path of the water.
  • Water is coming through a ceiling, light fixture or electrical outlet.
  • The house was flooded when you walked in, with no telling how many hours it had been going.
